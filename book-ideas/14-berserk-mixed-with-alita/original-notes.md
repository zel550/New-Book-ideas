# A Book Idea About Berserk Mixed With Alita — Original Notes

_Source: Google Drive file "a book idea about berserk mixed with alita.docx". Reproduced in full below._

## Full original text

A book about a boy, teenager, 16-17-18, in a postapocalyptic world sometime in the future, is basically powerless, dreams of going to a better place somewhere in the world (the "Castle on the hill" dream) finds a sword (the sword from "Berserk" but augmented with technology, so it follows the carriers flows with engines and exhausts, much like a rocket, so its lighter, quicker and easier to wield, at the same time does more damage); the sword (or a previous version of the sword) was wield by a mythical warrior (Berserk basically) who was consumed by his rage. Now this sword was built, but its too heavy for anyone to wield, until the boy finds him. The sword connects with the boy and a hidden power is awakened in it, it whirs and buzzes, yellow-red fiery light glows in it, its parts start moving, its engines working, fire comes from it (like the doctors hammer in Alita, similar weapon), the boy starts glowing too and adjusting, and both become one. This is achieved through a yellow crystal that the boy has embedded in its arm (corresponding to a yellow crystal in the sword's hilt) that makes them connect. Now the boy can wield the sword and can even call it to him when they are separated (like thors hammer)

At a point in the book, the boy and the sword are separated by enemies, the boy cant call it, because they've chained the sword down with powerful chains and are looking to extract the power from it. The boy is with somebody, also chained, says "I cant do it, Im nothing without the sword" the somebody replies "You have the same power, you idiot! the same power that is in the sword is also in you" The boy realizes that the power lives in him too, so he calls it into being, starts glowing (eyes and all) breaks his chains and breaks free the sword and everybody lives happily ever after

Additional ideas: Time is an ocean, not a river. Events don't unfold "before" or "after", but at the same time, just at different places in time. So this is a story, told by a grandpa to his boy, and it actually involves 3 or 4 storylines – that of the grandpa, of a samurai hundreds of years ago and of this boy, thousands of year in the future. In each of the stories the protagonists face the final opponent – the grandpa some thieves, one of which doesn't want to hurt him, the other wants to kill him, the grandpa kills the second thief instead with some technology. The samurai its some army that's attacking his city, the samurai dies; the boy it's the villain, who's superior technological body gives him the advantage, also the villain harnesses evil, dark energy, the opposite of the boy's energy. The events unfold simultaneously – the three heroes are knocked to the ground, but stand up and the events unfold at the same time – attacking and hurting/defeating their opponents and/or dying.

Idea 2: The boy with the sword in the future faces the final opponent (everybody's watching them) they fight, his opponent is much stronger and more powerful, he's taken all the power he can get for himself, defeats the boy (wounds him mortally) the boy dies, only to get up, his mechanical body falling apart to reveal a body of pure light. The villain gets very angry, eventually becomes a body of pure darkness. Both fight, the boy says to him that "When you die, everything you've taken will be taken away from you and everything that you've given will be given back to you." The villain tries to destroy the light, covering the world in darkness, and for a moment succeeds, only to find out that he can't exist without light (no light – no darkness, they are one and the same, part of the same system) so after defeating the boy made of light, he disappears, leaving the world as it was. The boy appears again and says to his friends, that he has to go too, his mission was successful (or something) but he can't stay. So his light spreads to the world (along with the darkness)

Idea 3: The boy the whole book dreams of becoming a real boy (Pinnochio archetype)
