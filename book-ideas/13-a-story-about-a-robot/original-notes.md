# A Story About a Robot — Original Notes

_Source: Google Drive file "A story about a robot.docx". Reproduced in full below._

## Full original text

Humanity creates a super-computer to find the purpose of existence. (maybe after a lot of failed attempts, because previous versions weren't powerful enough to do all the necessary computations) The computer finds out that existence is a result of a glitch and is meaningless. The big bang was a mistake, which caused enormous trouble. So, the computer realises that it must end existence itself, so that the problem is fixed. It needs to fix the bug, the glitch, so that everything goes back to normal.

Main protagonists – a scientist, programmer of sorts, engineer, and a meditator in a Buddhist temple. The protagonists seek for answers before creating the computer and after it, on how to stop it, or to prove it wrong.

And the end they find out they have been asking the wrong question – existence is purposeless, it's like a game. They need to ask a different question or somehow change the machine's perception. Problem is, the computer is made to find out the purpose of things – If a thing doesn't have a purpose, it is a mistake and must be removed. Maybe the computer realises it, too, doesn't really have a purpose, because, even if it manages to end existence itself, the process will simply start again.

The clock, that the computer has started, runs out and everyone is ready to die. But the world doesn't end.

"Are we dead?" someone asks.

"I feel… lighter" someone else says.

Everything is still there, but everybody feels lighter and free of their troubles, free of the notion that there is a separate "I". In a way, the old world ended and a new one began as everyone simultaneously experienced bliss by sudden awakening. That's what the computer was aiming to do from the start – it understood that the meaning of existence can't be explained, but felt and so it needed for everybody to believe that they were going to die, so they would let go of their egos and experience reality as it is – true bliss. In a way, the computer destroyed the world and created a new one by changing everyone's perception of it.
