# Magic Prison for Juveniles — Full Outline

## Logline
Sentenced to a magical juvenile detention facility that actively hunts its own inmates, a teenage sorceress convicted of a crime she didn't fully commit has to master forbidden magic fast enough to survive the building — while working out who really wants her dead.

## Genre
YA Dark Fantasy — Academy/Survival subgenre. Comp: *A Deadly Education* (Naomi Novik) crossed with *Orange Is the New Black*'s institutional politics and *The Maze Runner*'s survive-the-structure tension.

## Premise / World
The Cinder Wardship is a magical correctional facility for underage spellcasters — part prison, part school, built inside a collapsed ley-line nexus so unstable that the building itself generates hostile magical constructs ("Wardens") that stalk the halls, feeding on the inmates' raw magic. Officially, the Wardens are "rehabilitation trials." Unofficially, everyone knows the mortality rate is how the state keeps the facility's population — and its magic-drain output, which powers half the kingdom's grid — profitable. Inmates aren't taught to control their power so much as forced to survive it.

## Themes
- The juvenile justice system as a machine that manufactures the outcomes it claims to prevent
- Power as something extracted from the powerless (literal in this world: the facility drains inmates' magic to function)
- Found family among people the world has already written off
- The difference between being guilty and being convicted
- Growing into your power under a system designed to make sure you never fully control it

## Principal Characters
- **Rae Calloway** — 16, protagonist. Convicted of a lethal magical outburst she partially remembers; genuinely unsure how much of the blame is hers. Guarded, competent, allergic to trusting anyone.
- **Dorian Ashgrove** — Rae's assigned "cellmate"/survival partner, from a wealthy magical family, in for a crime that smells more like a cover-up for someone else's; initially insufferable, becomes Rae's closest ally.
- **Warden-Mistress Petra Voss** — the facility's administrator; not a mustache-twirling villain but a true believer in the system, which makes her scarier — and, late in the book, a more complicated obstacle than a simple antagonist.
- **Combs** — an older inmate, three years into an unusually long sentence, who's mapped the building's Warden patterns and runs an informal survival network/black market among inmates.
- **The Nexus** — the building itself, semi-sentient, generating Wardens; less a "villain" than an ecosystem the characters must learn to read and eventually renegotiate with.
- **Rae's little brother (offstage/flashback figure)** — the reason Rae's outburst happened in the first place; his safety is Rae's driving motivation to survive and get out.

## Structure: Three Acts, 28 Chapters

### Act One — Intake (Ch. 1–8)
1. Rae's sentencing and transport to the Cinder Wardship; the system's cold bureaucratic cruelty established immediately.
2. Orientation: the "rules" of survival, which read like prison intake crossed with a syllabus — Wardens patrol at set (and unset) intervals; magic use attracts them; you're assigned a cellmate for a reason.
3. Meets Dorian; instant friction — he's rich, entitled, clearly hiding something about his own case.
4. First Warden encounter — near-disaster, survived through luck and Combs' quiet intervention.
5. Rae starts learning the facility's real curriculum: not "rehabilitation" but conditioning kids to suppress their power to reduce Warden attraction — i.e., literally teaching them to be weaker.
6. Flashback interlude: what actually happened the night of Rae's outburst — the incident is muddier than the official record; something/someone provoked it.
7. Rae connects with Combs' network; learns the unofficial survival economy (favors, information, contraband minor magic items).
8. First real look at Warden-Mistress Voss — a private audience where Rae realizes Voss isn't cartoonishly evil, she genuinely believes this system saves more kids than it kills. Chilling for its sincerity.

### Act Two — The Nexus (Ch. 9–20)
9. Rae and Dorian begin quietly working together against the facility's official teaching — trying to understand their power rather than suppress it, at greater personal risk.
10. Rae discovers a pattern: the Wardens aren't random — they're drawn to specific magical "signatures," and the facility harvests the drained magic through a central relay tied to the kingdom's power grid.
11. Combs reveals their own secret: their sentence is arbitrarily extended every time they get close to fully understanding the Nexus — a system rigged never to let its best "students" leave.
12. Dorian's real backstory surfaces: he's covering for a family member; his crime isn't his own, a mirror to Rae's ambiguous guilt.
13. A near-fatal Warden attack forces Rae to use real, uncontrolled power for the first time inside the facility — she survives, but it flags her to Voss as "high yield," making her more valuable alive-but-contained, not free.
14. Rae is pulled into "special sessions" with Voss — increased privileges in exchange for higher-output magic use, an insidious trap dressed as reward.
15. Growing rift between Rae and Dorian over whether to play along (his instinct, being from money — survive quietly, get out early) or resist (Rae's instinct, driven by wanting to get back to her brother on her own terms).
16. Rae and Combs attempt to map the Nexus relay system, discovering it's not just draining magic — it's slowly killing the building's "core," and thus destabilizing containment on ALL the Wardens.
17. A facility-wide incident (a punishment, a riot, an especially bad Warden swarm) raises the stakes and costs a secondary character (ideally someone from Combs' network) their life or freedom, making the system's cost concrete.
18. Rae realizes the true shape of the choice: help stabilize the Nexus and prop up the system that's imprisoning her (safer, sanctioned), or destabilize it further to force a systemic collapse/reckoning (dangerous, uncertain, but might actually free everyone).
19. Dorian comes around to Rae's side after learning Voss has been quietly grooming him for early release in exchange for turning informant on Rae's group.
20. The group (Rae, Dorian, Combs, allies) commits to a plan: engineer a controlled overload of the Nexus during a mandated "trial" (a supervised Warden gauntlet), forcing an external inspection/expose while surviving it.

### Act Three — Breakout (Ch. 21–28)
21. Preparation chapter — gathering the specific magic components/knowledge needed, higher stakes as Voss grows suspicious of Rae's group.
22. Voss moves first: isolates Rae, offers her a genuine, tempting deal — protection and eventual freedom, at the cost of turning on her friends and helping the system run more "efficiently."
23. Rae's core test: refuses the deal, at real cost (punishment, isolation, or a forced high-risk solo Warden trial).
24. The trial arrives — a full facility "assessment" event, effectively a gauntlet in front of state inspectors, meant to showcase the system's success.
25. Rae and the others execute their plan mid-trial: rather than suppress their power to survive quietly, they let it flare in a coordinated overload, exposing the relay/harvesting system to the visiting inspectors in real time.
26. The Nexus, destabilized, turns fully chaotic — a last, most dangerous Warden surge that the group must survive together, using everything they've learned, not suppression but mastery.
27. Voss's true colors (or true tragedy) resolve — either she doubles down and is taken down by her own system, or she has a last-minute crisis of conscience that costs her something to help them, depending on how sympathetic the ending should land. Recommended: she's arrested along with the state officials who funded the harvesting scheme, but gets one line acknowledging she believed she was helping — no clean villain, just complicity.
28. Aftermath: facility exposed, reform (not perfect, not total) begins; Rae is released/paroled under new terms; final image of Rae reunited with her brother, magic no longer something to hide, but power she now understands and controls on her own terms.

## Craft Notes
- Keep the Wardens genuinely scary and mechanically consistent (like a videogame's enemy AI) so survival sequences have real stakes and rules the reader can track.
- Voss should never become a cartoon — her sincerity is what makes the institutional critique land.
- Series potential: Book 2 could follow systemic reform failing/backsliding, with Rae now an advocate/whistleblower navigating a system that resists actually changing.
