# The Boy and the Enchantress — Original Notes

_Source: Google Drive brainstorm document. Reproduced in full below._

## Full original text

The book starts with two boys, who live In a village, somewhere between the year 1000-1500 A.D. (after Christ anyways). Simple village, people hunt, grow crops etc. to feed themselves. The boys play in the forest, climb trees, rocks, etc, have friends, the life in the village is shown.

Then something happens. Once every month the bowls of the earth move. It starts as an earthquake, the ground starts shifting to reveal an enormous cavern in the middle of a field. From the cavern machinery appears – an underground, technologically advanced city, thousands of year ahead of the villagers. It is an event, which happens once a month (a year, ten years maybe?), and the opening to the city remains there only for 24 hours, after which it closes. Legend says if you don't manage to make it back in that time, youll never be heard from again. The villagers however use that opportunity to procure technology, they otherwise they don't have access too – medicine and other stuff, not very advanced things, as they do not have the resources to afford them. They just shop from markets a couple of hours near the surface, as they don't want to risk going too far in case they don't make it back. A tragic event has happened before, where someone went in (a couple of people, a group etc., somebody's husband maybe, the father of one of the boys perhaps, Motivation?) and they didn't come back. The boys aren't allowed in, only adults. However they sneak in and decide to explore. They have gathered enough resources to buy from the market some trinket that does a simple trick (simple in terms of the technologically advanced city) like shining a light or something like that. However one of the boys gets distracted, starts following something in the shadows, it turns out it's a little girl, she steals the gadget, he runs after her, his friend runs after him, they are lost, try to find their way back, before they can make it, the opening closes.
They are stuck in the city.

The Enchantress: Somewhere in the book they meet a woman, The Enchantress. They meet her at a most desperate time, where all hope is lost. She comes in and illuminates the dark (figuratively and metaphorically) performs some sort of spectacular magic to destroy all of their current foes and saves them. She grants them some sorts of gifts or advice that shows the path ahead.
Someone dies, the main character (the boy) asks her through tears for her to return his life, but she says she can't. Here, she explains a major theme in the book: Life and Death exist in balance, also good and evil, light and darkness. You could have one without the other, its like energy – which is a wave, and a wave has crests and trophs. In the same way, if she disturbs the balance of reality too much it may cause enormous harm, as every good act she does will correspond with an evil act happening elsewhere. The time where she saved the two boys was an enormous exception. Other than that, she doesn't use her powers except for trivial things. Except, at the end of the book (almost) she sacrifices herself, does some spectacular (even more spectacular than before) magic to help or save the main character, who then proceeds to defeat the bad guy, escape from the city, save the little girl or whatever. The Enchantress dies, sacrificing her life, so that balance, she upset with her good act, is restored.

The Boy has to face the Villain in the same way a hero faces the dragon to get the gold. The gold is representative of what the scary monster has to offer to the hero as a reward, which then the Hero, after sacrificing something valuable to him, incorporates within his frame structure. The Boy's reward is peace with the fact that he lost his father – he doesn't get him back in the end, but get's to say goodbye to him and live on. He incorporates his father's values in himself after defeating The Villain – the most scary thing where The Boy least wants to look.

The boy wants to save his dad but actually the real reason why he wants to find him is because he has hate and anger towards him for leaving him and wants to know why. When he finds him by the end, The Villain makes him reveal his true feelings to his dad. They have an honest talk and then the boy has to let the dad go.
