A village has to sacrifice a young woman to an evil dragon or be destroyed. You've heard the story. But not this one.

You've heard the story – a village has to give a dragon a virgin girl every year or be incinerated, blah, blah. But this story goes different.

## Author's follow-up notes (dictated in conversation, 2026-07-31)

_Reproduced as given, lightly cleaned up for punctuation only — content and word choice are the author's own._

Book begins with the trial of the girl for releasing the dragon or killing someone, I'm not sure who, but through the testimony of witnesses it is revealed her story; at the end it turns out one of the attendees is the dragon shapeshifting; or, alternatively, the group of misfits she finds in the dark forest are in the attendees of the trial, hidden, and reveal themselves at the dramatically appropriate moment to save her and go into the last 25 percent of the story, maybe saving the dragon who is about to be killed or something.

So it's her best friend, a fair beautiful maiden, that is going to go to the dark forest, from which there is no return, to be eaten by the dragon; but instead, our protagonist, who has sworn vengeance on the dragon, because he did something bad before, like attacked the village or something, takes her place. She hides behind a rock and swaps place with the girl, puts on her red cloak — this is a Red Hood, or whatever the girl retelling. From her parents' POV, who are watching this other girl walk towards the forest, the girl stumbles, falls behind a rock (actually pulled by our protagonist, who plotted this with the sacrifice girl before), then gets up, but her walk is weird; parents realise it's their girl and call out her name — she turns around to look at them with her blue eyes, just before entering the dark forest and being irretrievably lost, since no one comes back from the dark forest, and the villagers can't come in.

Once inside, she goes on the path toward the dragon — she just needs to follow the path. She has trained with someone from the village in secret, trained herself to be able to kill the dragon with sword, spear, etc. She meets companions — first, she meets a goblin that is being persecuted by other goblins (goblins are the servants of the dragon, who were supposed to pick her up and bring her to the dragon); she saves the goblin, chases the other goblins away; the persecuted goblin is very intelligent and explains he no longer wants to serve the dragon — she says she is here to kill the dragon; the goblin decides to join her since she saved his life. Next, they meet a samodiva, a female spirit of the forest, a beautiful woman, who is part of a group of samodivas, but she wants to join to kill the dragon as well, against the wishes of her tribe. Then they meet the Wolf, a lone mercenary, actually an anthropomorphic wolf, who also joins them.

In their quest, they visit the house of the forest witch, Baba Yaga, who they hope is going to help them defeat the dragon; watching the witch go down the stairs of her house on chicken legs, they are struck by her beauty, except the Wolf, who cringes and says she looks disgusting, since she has no hair on her face etc. — to him all humans are disgusting, like naked monkeys.

The witch tells them the dragon's been trapped by a magic spell; she has something to do with that — I think she dies, but I'm not sure. She has stone golems protecting her house. She gives the girl protagonist a stone golem in a miniature figure, which can expand to a full-size giant golem when she speaks a magic word.

They end up going to the dragon, they are going to fight him, but he laughs — the Wolf has betrayed her, the Wolf is working for the dragon; the Wolf is ashamed and hides. She offers herself as sacrifice to be eaten by the dragon, goes into his maw; in there, she speaks the magic words, the golem grows to his full size, the dragon heaves, can't move, has to spit her out (Red Riding Hood analogy to the Wolf).
