# Girl Kills the Dragon — Full Outline

## Logline
Chained to a rock and left for a dragon everyone swears is a monster, a sharp-tongued village outcast discovers the sacrifice is a centuries-old scam — and that surviving "girls" have been quietly running the resistance from inside the dragon's lair ever since.

## Genre
YA Fantasy / Fairy-Tale Retelling — Coming of Age, Comedy. Tone: *Damsel* meets *The Princess Bride*, with the self-aware humor of *Shrek* and the found-family warmth of *Howl's Moving Castle*.

## Themes
- Who benefits from a "tradition," and who pays for it
- Reclaiming a story that's been told about you instead of by you
- Found family and female solidarity as the real antidote to sacrifice culture
- Growing up = realizing the adults in charge were never as in-control as they seemed
- Comedy as a survival skill — laughing at the thing that's supposed to terrify you

## Principal Characters
- **Wren Ashcombe** — 17, our protagonist. Village misfit, too blunt for polite company, secretly furious that she's spent her whole life being told to be smaller. Selected as this year's "Chosen Bride of the Mountain."
- **Ember** — the dragon. NOT a monster: ancient, dryly funny, deeply tired of the arrangement, and — twist — was tricked into this role generations ago by the village council in exchange for protection magic. Doesn't eat the girls; trains them.
- **Sable, Marlowe, Junip** — three previous "sacrifices," now grown women living secretly in the dragon's mountain, running supply lines and sabotage against the village council. Wren's found family / mentors.
- **Lord Ossian Vane** — the village's smiling, reasonable-seeming steward, actual architect of the sacrifice scam; uses the "dragon tax" to extort the village and eliminate inconvenient young women (troublemakers, unmarriageable daughters, witnesses).
- **Tam** — Wren's childhood best friend, a village guard-in-training, torn between duty and loyalty to Wren; comic relief with a real arc.

## Structure: Three Acts, 26 Chapters

### Act One — The Sacrifice (Ch. 1–7)
1. Cold open: the "Choosing" festival — false pageantry, forced smiles. Wren is picked, to no one's surprise (she's too outspoken, too poor, too inconvenient).
2. Village life in miniature: we see Lord Vane's oily control, Wren's dead-end prospects, Tam's helpless guilt.
3. The procession to the mountain — equal parts funeral and parade. Wren's internal voice is dry, furious, funny.
4. Chained to the rock. Wren, expecting death, instead delivers a scathing monologue to the empty sky out of pure spite.
5. Ember arrives — not what anyone described. First real conversation: Ember explains, reluctantly, that she doesn't eat anyone, and asks Wren the question no one ever thinks to ask a "victim": "Do you want to go home?"
6. Wren is brought into the mountain and meets Sable, Marlowe, and Junip — the "eaten" girls, alive, thriving, running an underground network.
7. The truth: decades ago, Vane's ancestor struck a coerced bargain with Ember (a geas/magical debt) — she protects the valley from something worse in exchange for "tribute," which the council quietly twisted into human sacrifice for its own profit and population control.

### Act Two — The Mountain (Ch. 8–19)
8. Wren refuses to just hide out — she wants to go back and expose Vane. The others warn her: they've tried before; no one believes escaped "sacrifices," they're branded witches or liars.
9. Training montage (played partly for comedy): Ember and the others teach Wren real self-sufficiency — combat, forging, reading Vane's ledgers, dragon-lore, and how to lie confidently to men in charge.
10. We learn Ember's own stall: she's magically bound to the mountain by the original geas and literally cannot leave to confront Vane herself — she needs human hands to do it.
11. A supply run into the village goes sideways — Wren, in disguise, nearly reveals herself to Tam.
12. Tam figures out something is wrong with the official story and starts investigating independently — parallel comic-suspense thread.
13. Wren finds a cache of Vane's private letters proving he's been selecting "inconvenient" girls (witnesses to his corruption, unmarried pregnant women, political rivals' daughters) rather than truly random ones.
14. Character deepening: Sable, Marlowe, and Junip's backstories — why each stayed, what they lost, why they keep fighting anyway (mostly through banter, not tragedy dumps).
15. Ember and Wren's relationship deepens; Ember, centuries old and lonely, starts to see Wren as something like a daughter/protégé — comic-tender scenes.
16. Tam discovers the mountain network himself, chasing Wren; instead of betraying them, he's horrified by what he learns about Vane and joins.
17. A near-disaster: Vane sends soldiers to "recheck" the sacrifice site after suspicious village rumors; the group barely avoids exposure.
18. Wren proposes the plan: instead of hiding, stage a public reveal at the next Choosing festival — force Vane to either confess or attack a dragon in front of the whole village.
19. Preparation chapter — gathering evidence, allies among sympathetic villagers, and Ember quietly testing the limits of her binding to see how much she can actually do when it counts.

### Act Three — The Reckoning (Ch. 20–26)
20. The next Choosing festival. Wren returns publicly, alive, in front of the whole village — chaos, disbelief, Vane's forced smile cracking.
21. Vane tries to spin it (she "escaped the dragon's mercy," dangerous, must be silenced) and moves to have her seized.
22. Wren produces the ledgers/evidence in front of the crowd; Tam backs her from within the guard.
23. Vane, cornered, reveals his own desperation — tries to invoke the geas himself, order the "dragon" to attack Wren as proof of the old story, exposing that he's been controlling the narrative, not the dragon, all along.
24. Ember appears — not to attack, but to publicly refuse Vane's order, breaking the illusion of the "monster" in front of everyone and confirming Wren's account.
25. Climactic confrontation: Vane, out of moves, tries something drastic (violence, a hostage situation, or invoking older, darker magic tied to the original geas) — Wren, Ember, Sable/Marlowe/Junip, and Tam take him down together, no one hero saving the day alone.
26. Resolution: Vane deposed/exposed; the village rebuilds its relationship with Ember out in the open (tribute becomes trade, not sacrifice); Wren chooses to keep one foot in the mountain and one in the village — no longer an outcast, but not fully "settled" either. Final beat: a new girl is nervous about the next Choosing; Wren gets to be the one who tells her the truth this time.

## Craft Notes
- Keep the comedic voice front and center in Act 1–2 (banter, Wren's internal snark) so the Act 3 stakes land as earned rather than tonally jarring.
- Ember should never fully lose her scariness/otherness even once she's revealed as an ally — she's a friend, not a pet dragon.
- Series potential: Book 2 could follow the "trade, not tribute" system straining under a new threat, with Wren now on the other side of power.
