# Bystanders — Full Outline

## Logline
While her roommate lives out a swoony, over-the-top will-they-won't-they with the broody guy next door, a young woman quietly racing to break a century-old family curse keeps getting dragged into the "main" romance plot happening around her — until the two storylines collide and the rom-com turns out to be the key to her Quest all along.

## Genre
Literary Fantasy / Genre-Bending Comedy — a structural experiment: one "background" romantic-comedy plotline and one "foreground" dark-fantasy Quest plotline, told from the Quest character's point of view, so the reader experiences the rom-com the way a real bystander would — bewildered, exasperated, occasionally moved despite themselves.

## Concept / Structural Hook
Every story has main characters and background characters — the friend who exists to deliver one good line, the neighbor who's only there for a meet-cute. This book puts a *Quest protagonist* inside someone else's *romance protagonist*'s story and asks: what does the "B-plot" character's actual life look like, off-page, while all that main-character nonsense is happening two feet away from them?

## Themes
- Whose story counts as "the story" — and what gets treated as backdrop
- Romantic love as one genre convention among many, not the default setting of life
- Found family vs. romantic love as the thing that actually saves you
- The comedy and cost of being someone else's supporting character
- Real stakes (a curse, a deadline, a family debt) vs. performed stakes (will they kiss)

## Principal Characters
- **Nora Kessler** — 27, our protagonist and narrator. Last of a family line bound to an old curse: she has to complete a specific, strange, escalating set of tasks (a Quest, initially framed almost like a scavenger hunt from her late grandmother) before her thirtieth birthday, or the curse passes to a child she doesn't have yet — meaning it just... waits, dormant, malevolent, for the family to continue. Sharp, private, allergic to melodrama.
- **Yasmin Cole** — Nora's roommate and best friend, the "main character" of the romance plot. Warm, chaotic, narrates her own life like a movie trailer. Not stupid — just genuinely living in a different genre.
- **Julian Marsh** — the broody neighbor, Yasmin's love interest. Standard smoldering-love-interest surface... except Nora starts noticing details that don't fit a rom-com at all: he keeps specific old texts, knows things about the Kessler family curse he shouldn't, and reacts oddly to items on Nora's Quest list.
- **Grandmother Elke Kessler (deceased, via letters/flashback)** — designed the Quest/curse-breaking ritual; appears through cryptic instructions that read like classic fairy-tale tasks but turn out to be coded, practical steps.
- **Combs the cat / a talking familiar (optional whimsical device)** — Nora's one confidant who knows about the curse, providing a wry outside voice on both plotlines.

## Structure: Three Acts, 26 Chapters

### Act One — Two Plots, One Apartment (Ch. 1–7)
1. Cold open: Yasmin has an aggressively cinematic meet-cute with Julian in the building hallway (spilled coffee, eye contact, swelling metaphorical music) while Nora, holding a box of grave dirt from task #1 of her Quest, has to awkwardly step around them.
2. We get Nora's real plot: her grandmother's will included a locked box, a list of seven impossible-sounding tasks, and a deadline — her 30th birthday, four months away.
3. Establish the comedic tension: every time Nora tries to focus on her Quest, she's interrupted by Yasmin needing a wing-woman, a shoulder to cry on, or an audience for the Julian Saga.
4. Nora completes Quest Task #1 (something like "retrieve what was buried under the oldest tree in the city") in the middle of the night — intercut with Yasmin's dramatic 2 a.m. phone call about whether Julian's texting style means he's "emotionally unavailable."
5. First real hint something's off with Julian: Nora spots him doing something inexplicable (buying an item from her Quest list at a market; speaking a phrase from her grandmother's letters).
6. Nora tries to warn Yasmin without revealing her own secret; Yasmin, mid-romance-plot, reads it as jealousy or interference, not concern — genre miscommunication as comedy and real hurt.
7. Nora completes Task #2, badly, nearly getting hurt, entirely alone — juxtaposed against Yasmin and Julian's first "real" date, shown only in the fragments Nora overhears through the wall.

### Act Two — Crossed Wires (Ch. 8–19)
8. Nora's Quest tasks escalate in strangeness and danger (a task requiring a favor from a stranger who "owes" the Kessler line; a task requiring something only obtainable by trading something precious).
9. Julian starts showing up in Nora's plot too directly to be coincidence — he clearly knows about the curse; is he protecting her, working against her, or something else?
10. A rom-com set piece (a group dinner, a party) is shown almost entirely through Nora's exhausted, task-list-running internal monologue — the comedic core chapter of the book, contrasting genre registers on the page.
11. Yasmin senses Nora is hiding something and, in a moment that breaks the "background character" framing, actually asks real, searching questions — reminder that side characters have inner lives their "main characters" rarely bother to access.
12. Nora, cornered, tells Yasmin a partial truth about the curse. Yasmin's reaction subverts expectation: instead of making it about herself, she pivots hard into being genuinely useful — research, alibis, moral support.
13. Julian reveals himself: his family has a historical, complicated tie to the Kessler curse (maybe an ancestor who helped create it, or who broke a previous cycle at great cost) — he's not dangerous, but he's not neutral either.
14. The romance plot and Quest plot start to visibly interlock: a Quest task requires something in Julian's family's possession, meaning Yasmin's love interest becomes a literal obstacle/ally in Nora's story.
15. Tension: Nora has to decide whether to use Yasmin's relationship with Julian to advance her Quest — a real moral question about instrumentalizing your best friend's "main plot" for your "side plot" needs.
16. Nora refuses to manipulate the relationship, choosing honesty over expediency, costing her time on the Quest clock.
17. Consequence: the refusal nearly costs Nora a critical task; Julian, moved by her choice, offers to help directly instead — genuine alliance, not romance (deliberately keeping Julian's arc tied to Yasmin, not triangulating with Nora).
18. Deepest Quest lore chapter: we learn what the curse actually is/does, why Elke structured it as a scavenger hunt (protecting a younger, more vulnerable version of the ritual's true purpose — e.g., not doom, but the sealing-away of something the family once loosed on the world).
19. Ticking-clock pressure mounts: Nora has two tasks left and three weeks; Yasmin and Julian's relationship hits its genre-mandated "third act breakup" moment right as Nora needs Julian's help most.

### Act Three — Main Character Energy (Ch. 20–26)
20. Nora has to choose: chase her final Quest tasks alone on schedule, or show up for Yasmin during the "breakup" beat, the exact kind of moment she's spent the whole book treating as background noise.
21. Nora shows up for Yasmin anyway — and in doing so, discovers Yasmin and Julian's conflict is actually rooted in the same secrecy/honesty theme as her own arc; her advice to them doubles as her own growth.
22. Final Quest task requires a public, exposed act — Nora can no longer keep her story separate/background; it collides visibly with the "main" plot (possibly at a wedding, party, or public event where Yasmin and Julian's story is also reaching its climax).
23. Simultaneous climax: Yasmin and Julian's romantic resolution plays out in the same scene as Nora's final ritual — structurally, the book finally lets both plots be "main" at once, in the same frame.
24. The curse-breaking ritual demands genuine sacrifice/cost (not death, but something real — a possession, a relationship, a piece of family history) — Nora completes it, aided unexpectedly by Yasmin, who finally gets to be someone else's main character for a scene.
25. Resolution of the romance plot (recommend: earned, but slightly subverted — not a perfect fairy-tale ending, acknowledging the genre while not fully satirizing it) alongside resolution of the curse.
26. Coda: Nora, freed of the Quest, has to figure out what her life is when it's not organized around a deadline — final image reframes the "bystander" idea: everyone is the background character in someone else's story and the main character in their own, simultaneously.

## Craft Notes
- Structurally alternate close chapters on Nora's Quest with "overheard" fragments of Yasmin's romance, gradually shortening the distance between them until Act 3 merges the two.
- Play the tonal clash for real comedy in Act 1–2, but let it earn sincerity by Act 3 — the joke should deepen into genuine warmth, not undercut it.
- Julian must never become a romantic rival for Nora — the point is two protagonists sharing space, not a love triangle.
